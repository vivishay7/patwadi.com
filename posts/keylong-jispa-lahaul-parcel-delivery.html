Parcel delivery to Keylong, Jispa and Lahaul: what to know before sending

Sending a parcel to Keylong, Jispa, Sissu, Darcha or deeper Lahaul is not the same as sending a parcel to a normal city. The route depends on road status, vehicle availability, pickup timing, local handoffs, and whether the receiver can collect from an agreed point.

By Patwadi Logistics LLP · Updated 4 Jul 2026 · 7 min read
Before you send

Parcels to Keylong, Jispa and Lahaul should always be confirmed before dispatch. Road status, weather, and operational windows vary. Always confirm route, handoff point, and delivery window on WhatsApp first — not after the parcel has left.

Lahaul is now year-round accessible — but that does not mean year-round easy

The Atal Tunnel, opened in October 2020, changed Lahaul fundamentally. Before it, the region was cut off from Manali and the rest of Himachal for approximately six months every winter. The Rohtang Pass closes; Lahaul closes with it. The tunnel eliminated that seasonal isolation. Manali to Keylong is now accessible every month of the year.

What the tunnel did not eliminate: snowfall above the Sissu exit, landslides on the Tandi-Keylong stretch, road works, police controls during high tourist season, and the general reality that Lahaul’s roads are high-altitude mountain roads. Year-round accessible is not the same as year-round predictable.

For parcel delivery, this matters in a specific way: you can send to Keylong in January, which was impossible before 2020. But the delivery window still needs to be confirmed against current road status, not assumed from a calendar.

Why delivery to Lahaul is different

A parcel moving from Manali to Keylong may pass through the Atal Tunnel exit at Sissu, continue along the Chandra River valley through Tandi, and reach Keylong, the district headquarters. From there, Jispa is another 26 kilometres toward Darcha. Each segment has its own road condition, its own traffic pattern, and its own population density for local handoffs.

The right question is not simply “does a courier company serve the PIN code?” The better question is: is there an active route today, who is handling the parcel at each point, where will the handoff happen, and what is the plan if the receiver is not available at an isolated hill address?

What national courier networks typically offer here

Most national courier networks list Lahaul locations as extended or extra-service areas, served from hubs like Kullu or Manali rather than from Keylong itself. Standard delivery ETAs to Keylong from major platforms typically show 3 to 7 working days — which reflects the operational reality that Lahaul is not a primary service area for networks built around volume and plains coverage.

Delhivery and Blue Dart have similarly limited HP hill coverage. India Post reaches Keylong but on speeds consistent with remote government postal operations. None of these are wrong exactly — Lahaul is genuinely hard to serve. But “hard to serve” is not the same as “not worth serving.” Hotels, homestays, camps, shops, and residents in Lahaul all have parcel needs. Most of these get solved through personal connections, local agents, and conductor arrangements — systems that work within existing relationships but offer no tracking, no formal handoff record, and no accountability if something goes wrong.

Who this is for

  • Homestays, camps, cafés and hotels in Keylong, Jispa or Sissu needing supplies or forgotten item returns
  • Local shops moving small stock, samples, documents or replacement items between Lahaul and Manali/Kullu
  • Travellers sending bags, gear, jackets, chargers, or documents left behind in Manali or Keylong
  • Families sending care packages, books, medicines where permitted, or seasonal household items
  • Businesses moving cartons between Manali, Kullu, Keylong and nearby Himachal towns

Routes to ask about

  • Manali to Sissu — through Atal Tunnel, year-round subject to conditions
  • Manali to Keylong — Lahaul district headquarters, year-round via tunnel
  • Manali to Jispa — beyond Keylong toward Darcha, route-dependent
  • Kullu / Bhuntar to Keylong — longer leg via Mandi and Manali
  • Keylong to Jispa — short corridor within Lahaul
  • Keylong to Darcha — where available and road-confirmed
  • Lahaul to Manali / Kullu return movement — active on matching corridors

When doorstep may not be the best option

In Lahaul, doorstep delivery to a specific house number is often slower and more expensive than a confirmed landmark handoff. In many Lahaul locations, the practical delivery point is a known hotel desk, shop, bus stand, main-road point, or local contact. Patwadi confirms this before the parcel moves — so the sender and receiver both know exactly what to expect, and there is no ambiguity at the handoff.

Road and weather

Lahaul routes can be affected by snowfall at higher elevations, landslides on the Tandi-Keylong stretch, road works, police controls during tourist season, and conditions on segments above the tunnel exit. The Keylong-to-Kaza route via Kunzum Pass has its own seasonal status. Patwadi confirms road status and route availability before accepting a booking. A delivery commitment is only made when the route is confirmed as operational, not assumed.

What to send before booking

Share the route, pickup point, drop point, receiver phone number, nearest landmark or collection reference, parcel type, weight, dimensions, urgency, and whether a local handoff point works for the receiver. The more specific the receiver context, the faster the route can be confirmed.
